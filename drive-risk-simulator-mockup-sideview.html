Scene layers (back → front)

  • Sky gradient · sun or moon + stars.
  • Clouds — 2 or 3, drifting slowly to the left.
  • Two hill silhouettes, far one lighter than the near one.
  • Trees on the grass line, tilted a few degrees when the wind is strong.
  • Ground: grass strip → asphalt road → dirt below.
  • Car, fixed at ~26% from the left, wheels sitting on the road.
  • Weather overlay: rain, wet sheen, night tint, headlight beam.
  • UI overlay: environment pills top, countdown bottom-right.

    • Build notes — Flame canvas

  • Sky, grass, road, dirt: four RectangleComponents stacked by priority. No sprites required.
  • Motion = lane dashes, clouds and hills scrolling left; the car never moves. A ParallaxComponent handles all three at different speeds.
  • Scroll speed is tied to the slider value, so the scene visibly speeds up as km/h rises.
  • Rain: ~40 short white lines looping down. Fog: one translucent white rectangle. Night: one dark rectangle + a triangle headlight beam.
  • Uphill / downhill: rotate the whole world layer by ±4°, and scale it slightly so no gap shows at the corners. Nothing else changes.
  • Car: one simple side-view sprite, ~160 × 72 logical px — body silhouette, two windows, door line, two wheels with rims, one headlight, one taillight. Ten flat shapes, no gradients.

    • Build notes — Flutter shell

  • Everything outside the canvas is plain Flutter: Column + Card + Slider + Switch.
  • The Flame GameWidget sits inside the simulation container only, clipped to radius 18.
  • Environment pills are a Wrap of chips overlaid with Stack — add or drop a feature without touching the layout.
  • Risk colour comes from one function colorFor(score); it drives the rail, the card, the meter and the slider fill.
  • On high risk only, the canvas gets a 2px red outline — the warning stays contained instead of flooding the screen.

    State loop

  • Every 10s the simulator picks a new environment state; the countdown sits bottom-right of the canvas.
  • That state plus current speed and seatbelt goes to the model as one feature row.
  • The returned score animates over ~400ms so the change is visible on camera during a demo.
  • The input strip at the bottom shows the exact row being sent — it keeps the model visible, not just its output.
